## What We'll Do In Class


### SuperStore Sales - Exploration

We'll play with some fictional transaction data, which you can download here:  [Super Store Sales](./data/SuperStoreUS-2015.xlsx)

First, we'll have a class discussion about data and how it's organized. Every time
we get a new dataset to play with, we'll want to answer some fundamental questions:

- Provenance and Trust:
    - Where does this data come from?
    - Does it come from someone who has an agenda? Does someone benefit from
    us seeing this data in this format?
- Structure:
    - Do we understand every column?
    - Does the data type of each column make sense?
    - Granularity: What does a single row in the data tell us?
    - Is this data a single table? Are there multiple sheets? How do they relate to each other?
- Content:
    - How many rows are we working with?
    - Do we believe that this data is complete and accurate?
    - What information is included in this data?
    - What might be missing from this data?
    - What kinds of anomalies might exist in this dataset?
- Purpose:
    - What kinds of things might we learn?
    - What decisions might this data inform?

We'll talk through these as a group today, but you'll eventually do this
on your own. I'll want you to build a spidey
sense for independently thinking through these questions for every dataset
we study.


### Data Dashboarding

Pretend that you are a data analyst employed by SuperStore.

The CEO of your company is busy, and needs to be kept up to date on how their
business is doing. It's your job to provide that information.

What do you think the CEO needs to know? 

You'll create a new tab in this workbook called 'CEO_Dashboard', and populate it with whatever kind of summary information you think is important. 

Your dashboard should include data and text summarizing what
the data means. Make it look nice!

At minimum, I'd like you to demonstrate that you are comfortable with these functions:
- SUM
- COUNT
- MIN
- MAX
- UNIQUE (and/or COUNT(UNIQUE()))
- SUMIF
- COUNTIF

As before, upload your completed workbook to Github. We'll start next class by sharing what we came up with.
