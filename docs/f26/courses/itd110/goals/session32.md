## Lesson Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should:

- Understand the role of video and iframes in websites
- Be able to describe the difference between lossy/lossless compression
- Be able to use image maps on your website

### iFrames and Video

We'll quickly discuss iFrames and videos. I recommend that you practice by adding both to your website, but I won't make this a graded classwork assignment.

Just like with images - it's important that you respect copyright on any videos
you insert into your site. A good place to search for free videos is [Pixabay Videos](https://pixabay.com/videos/).

### Codecs and Compression

We'll have a short discussion about **codecs**, and why there are many different
types of compression. We'll categorize compression into two types:
- Lossless (we'll play with [this simulator](https://studio.code.org/s/text-compression/lessons/1/levels/2) to understand how lossless compression works)

### Activity - Image Map

Today's classwork is about image maps. My favorite use of them is for easter eggs. I've added one to my class website here: [https://cjonesexample.github.io/](https://cjonesexample.github.io/) - try clicking on the cat's nose.

Start by reviewing [the Edube page here](https://edube.org/learn/web-dev-ess-html/image-maps-1?action=page#understanding-image-maps), then check out [the w3schools page](https://www.w3schools.com/htmL/html_images_imagemap.asp), which has a lot of good examples. Then add your own fun easter egg to your own website. Choose any picture on your homepage and add an image map with a link that directs somewhere fun.

Note that this does not work with responsive images (ie any image that has the `srcset` and `sizes` attributes). If you select an image that uses those attributes, you'll need to remove them for this assignment.
