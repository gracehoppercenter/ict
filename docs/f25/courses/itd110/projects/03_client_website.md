# Client Website Project: Agile Web Development

## Project Overview
In this project, design and build a website for a real client. The website will include at least three HTML pages, with consistent styling. You'll 
apply the "agile software development" strategy - working
work through multiple iterations, gathering feedback and making improvements based on your client's needs. 

## Your Client
Choose someone who needs a simple website but doesn't have the skills to build it themselves. Good options include:
- A family member with a hobby or small business
- A club or organization you're involved with
- A friend with a creative project or portfolio needs
- A community member with a cause they want to promote

For this project, your client is responsible for the content on the page, and you
are responsible for the design. It's okay if you need to use placeholders (e.g. text from <https://www.lipsum.com/>) while you're waiting for the client to provide content.

**Important:** You'll need to identify one specific person as your client, and they must be available for at least three check-ins. You can send your client a link to this page so that they understand what they're signing up for.


## Project Requirements

### Phase 1: Discovery & Planning (Week 1)
1. **Client Interview:** Meet with your client to talk about their goals. Here are some things you might want to ask:
   - Purpose of the website
   - Target audience
   - Must-have features/content
   - Visual preferences (colors, style, you might search with them and find some existing websites that they like)
   - Ask them to start gathering photos that they want to include, text that they want to add, etc
      - Make sure they understand they they'll need copyright for any photos, following the same rules that we've followed in class this year.
   - You have no responsibility to your client beyond the scope of this project. Make sure they understand that you'll be delivering HTML code on a github pages site. If they want additional updates after this project is completed, you might choose to work with them, or they could find another HTML developer to work with.

2. **Design Mockup:** Before writing any code, draw a picture of what you think the website will look like. You might choose to draw this picture on a paper or whiteboard (if so, take a photo that you can include in your notes). If you prefer to do this digitally, I really like <https://excalidraw.com/>, or you could just use a Google Slide.

   Your mockup must include and label the UI design elements we've studied.

### Phase 2: Iterative Development (Week 2)

**Check-in Requirements:**
Your first check-in with the client will be to share and discuss your mock-up. After that, you must document at least one more check-in where you share a working website. For each check-in, you should document:
- Date and method of meeting (in-person, video call, etc.)
- What you showed the client
- Their feedback (quotes or specific comments)
- Changes you agreed to make


### Phase 3: Final Delivery (Week 3)
Deliver the final website to your client, and incorporate any last-minute tweaks that they suggest. Have a conversation with your client about copying this code to their own github account (or any other hosting provider), and document any plans you come up with.

## Required Files & Structure

This project should all go inside your github repo in a new directory called `projects/client`. In there, I'll expect to see:

- `index.html`
- At least two other HTML pages
- `style.css`
- `process.md` - where you'll document the development process, include photos/screenshots of your design mock-ups, and document your check-ins.
- `media/` - where you'll include any images 

## Other Requirements

As always, you must understand all of the code that you submit. I haven't decided whether or not we'll do a writing exercise for this project, but I reserve the right to ask you to explain your code.

Your final website must meet the WCAG AA standard.

## Alternative

The downside to writing a custom HTML page is that the client might struggle if they want to make changes on their own in the future. If this is important to your client, you might consider exploring <https://wordpress.org/>, which is a very popular website building tool that has a nice interactive editing system. If you choose to explore Wordpress, you'll write less custom HTML, but I'll expect that your process is the same. And you'll be on your own to learn how Wordpress works.
