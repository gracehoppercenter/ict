## Wrapping Up

All of your projects have made great progress this year, and they're all far
from complete. As we wrap up the year, we need to start to think about making 
sure future teams have what they need to pick up where you left off.

Here are a few tasks that I'd like to see every team work on this week:

- **Today! Clean up your issues** - Work with your team to make an honest assessment 
of which issues you'll complete by the end of this school year and move them
into the "todo" column of your project tracker. Anything that you don't plan
to complete by the end of the year should go to the "backlog" column. I'll review
this list before class on Thursday and make sure I agree with your decisions.
- **Curate your backlog** - Once you've agreed on what you will/won't do this year,
make sure that you're leaving a nice todo list for the next team. Review
your backlog to make sure it documents everything that you think needs to be included
before your project could be considered done, that all of the issues include enough
explanation for future teams to understand them, and that there aren't any duplicates.
- **Organize your repo** - Many of your repos are nested in weird structures. 
Cleanup to make sure that future teams won't need to dig around to find your files.
Your Django project shouldn't be nested - your repo homepage should include README.md,
manage.py, .gitignore, LICENSE, and requirements.txt all at the same level.
[The cosmetology repo](https://codeberg.org/GHC_Students/cosmetology_webapp) is
a good example of the structure I expect.
- **Clean up your README** - Your README file should describe the current state
of the project. Include a summary of the functionality, screenshots of the 
important screens, any information 
that future developers will need to know in order to run your project (eg about
environment variables, or email accounts), and a note that future developers can
find open issues via the `Project` screen and process notes in the wiki.
- **Clean up your wiki** - The README documents the functionality, and the wiki
should document process. Make sure it's well-organized, documents any 
brainstorming, sketches, etc you made. Also be sure that you have good notes from
all of the conversations.
- **Final customer meeting** - Your customer deserves a final presentation. Let
them know what you got done this year, and what you've left for a future team. 
Give them a chance to share final thoughts/feedback/ideas, and thank them for
being your customer!
- **Close all PRs** - We shouldn't go into the summer with any open PRs. Review 
all PRs and decide whether you'll merge them. If you aren't quite ready to merge,
close the PR and add a link from the issue explaining that you started working on
the PR but abandoned it.
