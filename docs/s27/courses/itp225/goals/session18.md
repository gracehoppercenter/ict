## Customers vs Stakeholders

Today, I'd like each group to spend some time thinking about who will use your
final project. This is an important step in 
[the ideation stage of software development](https://www.mendix.com/blog/agile-software-development-lifecycle-stages/).


Create a wiki page called 'Customers' where you'll document who
your customer is.

For this project, we'll break it down into two categories:

### Customer

We've already assigned a single point of contact who will be your 
"customer" for this project. We'll give them the final say on everything related
to what features you build, how you prioritize them, the look/feel/layout, etc. 

Our goal is that you'll meet with your customer at least once every two weeks. 
At each meeting you'll give them a walkthrough of the site so far (remember - 
the code in your `main` branch should always work and be ready for demos!), 
you'll get any feedback or suggestions they have about what you show them, and
you'll make a plan with them about what features you'll work on for the next 
two weeks. You should document all of these meetings in your project wiki.

One important thing to note - your customers don't know anything about Django!
When you meet with them, you'll want to focus on the user experience - not
the technical implementation. They might be interested in seeing your wireflow 
diagrams, and will certainly not be interested in reviewing your model diagrams.

### Stakeholders

Make a list of other people who should be
consulted as you work on your project. This list might be long, and it will
likely include:

- people who will have to use your system. This might even break into categories. 
will you have admin users?
- people who will be impacted by your system
- people who regulate your system (are there any relevant laws you need to follow?)
- people who will have to maintain your system in the future (please include Chris and Jeff on this list)

As you work on the ideation phase of this project, you'll want to talk to as 
many people on this list as you can. This will help you identify any important
requirements before you get too deep into your project. Make sure you document
each of these meetings in your wiki! 

You should review this list with your customer during your first meeting - they
will probably have more ideas.
